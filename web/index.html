Then a humanoid opens it

Hand the twin to a robot. A humanoid plans its stance and reach from the measured hinge, opens the real object in a separate simulation, and the grasp force shows whether the twin was right. With ObjectModel's twins all 10 doors, drawers and lids open; with a 2° axis error, 4 already fail.

Check every model in MuJoCo before export.

URDF and MJCF agree to 0.00 mm across the joint range, and 0.03 mm of replay error is left once tracking noise is removed.

Cancel out a hand-held camera.

Joints are fitted on motion relative to their parent, so 100% of 1 mm hand-held captures still validate.

Read metric scale from a printed marker.

A 50 mm ArUco square put the laptop's hinge within 3% of its true position.

Sweep every joint for collisions.

Each joint is driven through its whole range, and parts that would run into each other are flagged in the report.

Let a language model name the parts.

Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (Llama API, vLLM, Ollama) calls the lid a lid and picks aluminium. Its reply is checked; geometry never comes from it.

Five parts, one replay

Three drawers and a door, each found on its own and each with its own joint. Axis error 0.51° on average, and every drawer slides out of the body rather than into it.
